# CustomWorldgen for Minecraft 26.3 / Horizon

World generation data packs for Minecraft 26.3 (vanilla server or [Horizon](https://github.com/horizon-rust/horizon)):
big biomes, gentle plains for horses and building, huge mountains, ores in big single deposits, fewer tunnels.
The main pack is the `CustomWorldgen-26.3` folder; see below for the simple, lite and vanilla-terrain variants.

## Installing

1. Copy the `CustomWorldgen-26.3` folder into `server\<world name>\datapacks\`.
2. The world must be **new**. The pack only changes chunks that haven't been generated yet. In an existing world old areas stay as they were, and sharp steps appear at the border with new chunks. The pack also uses a different world height (up to Y 655).
3. Start the server. The log must not have data pack or registry errors. `/datapack list` should show `file/CustomWorldgen-26.3` among the enabled packs.
4. **Don't** put a 1.21.1 version of the pack into a 26.3 world: 26.3 can't read its format, and load errors can stop the world from starting.

Horizon picks up worldgen from `<world>\datapacks` itself (Horizon docs: `docs/world-generation.md`, the "Data packs" section). It logs what it couldn't apply from a pack. This pack adds no new biomes and does nothing vanilla 26.3 can't do.

## What's in the pack

| Goal | How | Values |
|---|---|---|
| Few tunnels | tunnel caves (spaghetti, noodle, cave entrances) stay only in their zones covering 2.3% of the map. Classic carver tunnels are 30 times rarer than vanilla | `TunnelKeepThreshold = -0.63`, `CarverScale.cave = 0.2 / 6` |
| Big caves rarer, but present | big cave halls ("cheese") and their pillars have their own zones on 14.1% of the map, independent of the tunnel zones. Canyons are 10 times rarer than vanilla | `BigCaveKeepThreshold = -0.35`, `CarverScale.canyon = 0.1` |
| Bigger biomes | climate noises (temperature, humidity, continentalness, erosion) are stretched | `ClimateOctaveShift = 3`, i.e. 8 times bigger |
| Smooth terrain for horses and building | plains, forests and meadows (erosion above -0.375) are almost flat: hills squashed to 1.5% of their height, small bumps smoothed 11 times, small lakes filled, rivers rarer. Vanilla terrain remains only in real mountains (erosion below -0.45) | `FlattenStrength = 0.985`, `MountainErosion = -0.45`, `FlatErosion = -0.375`, `FlatFactorBoost = 10` |
| Big mountains | in mountain zones whole massifs become peaks, mountains are higher, terrain is generated up to Y 512, building is possible up to Y 655 | `MassifStrength = 1`, `MountainAmplify = 0.7`, `TerrainCeiling = 512` |
| More witch huts and desert pyramids | placement grid of 14/4 chunks instead of vanilla 32/8, i.e. about 5 times more | `StructureSpacing` |
| Ores in big single deposits | every ore becomes a rare big deposit (10 blobs of size 64, ~3070 blocks). Ancient debris: one blob of ~150 blocks. Noise ore veins (iron with tuff, copper with granite) are off | `VeinSize`, `VeinBlobs`, `NoiseOreVeins = $false` |

Everything is generated by `build-pack.ps1` from the vanilla 26.3 files in `_vanilla-26.3` (not in this repository, see "Commands"). The parameters at the top of the script mean the same as in the 1.21.1 version.

## What changed from 1.21.1 to 26.3

In 26.3 Mojang reworked the worldgen format, and the 1.21.1 pack doesn't load in 26.3. The script now writes the new format:

- `pack.mcmeta`: format 121.
- Density functions: `left`/`right`/`input` instead of `argument1`/`argument2`/`argument`. `cache` instead of `flat_cache` and `cache_2d`. `gradient` instead of `y_clamped_gradient`.
- Noises: `base_octave`, `octave_count`, `amplitude_modifiers`, `base_amplitude` instead of `firstOctave`/`amplitudes`. `base_amplitude` is computed with vanilla's `NormalNoise` formula so the noises give the same values as in 1.21.1.
- `final_density` and `preliminary_surface_level` are now separate files. The pack changes the same things in them as it used to in `noise_settings`.
- Noise ore veins are now part of `material_rule`. The pack removes its two entries `copper_ore_vein` and `iron_ore_vein`.
- `configured_carver` became `carver`, `configured_feature` became `feature`, `config` was removed.
- 26.3 has no `random_patch`. A big deposit is now defined in `placed_feature`: `count 10` + an `offset` with a triangular spread of ±5. That's the same placement `random_patch` gave.
- `dimension_type` in the new format (with `attributes`) is taken from vanilla 26.3. The pack only changes the height: `height = 720`.
- The "ice region" option (`IceRegion`) was removed: it needed the Java mod More Density Functions, which doesn't work on Horizon. It was disabled anyway.

The old script is kept as `build-pack-1.21.1.ps1`. `tools\benchmark.ps1` uses it, because its test server is Fabric 1.21.1.

## Checks done (without running the game)

1. **26.3 format.** `tools\Check-Pack.ps1`: no key or type of the 1.21/26.2 format is left in the pack. Every density function, noise and feature the pack references exists in the pack or in vanilla 26.3. The script catches errors: given a broken reference and a file in the old format, it found both.
2. **Same logic as in 1.21.1.** The files of the working 1.21.1 pack were converted to 26.3 names and compared with the new ones. All 13 custom terrain functions (`offset`, `factor`, `depth`, `ridges`, `continents`, `erosion`, `flatness`, `lake_mask`, `massif`, `mountain_weight`, `offset_flat`, `ridges_rivers`, `ridges_stretched`) match exactly. The climate noises match (octaves, amplitudes, `base_amplitude`), and so do the carvers. `final_density` and `preliminary_surface_level` differ from vanilla 26.3 only by the `max(..., cave_suppressor)` (big caves) and `max(..., tunnel_suppressor)` (tunnels) wrappers and the moved top fade-out (Y 496..512).
   This check also found and fixed a bug: PowerShell passed `-1.0` as the string `"-1.0"`, and the game would have rejected `offset.json` and `ridges.json`.
3. **26.3 terrain = 1.21.1 terrain.** The vanilla `offset`/`factor`/`jaggedness` splines and terrain noises in 26.3 have the same numbers as in 1.21.1. So 1.21.1 benchmarks should be close for 26.3 too. But Mojang keeps the biome table (preset `minecraft:overworld`) in code, not data, and 26.3 added the biomes `pale_garden`, `dappled_forest`, `sulfur_caves`. So mountain sizes need to be measured again in the game itself (see below).
4. **Ore per chunk = vanilla.** Ore features (size, targets, placement) in 26.3 are the same as in 1.21.1, so the blocks-per-deposit measurements and `OreCorrection` adjustments from 1.21.1 still apply. An independent recount from the finished files shows that for every ore the average amount per chunk matches vanilla within 0.005%:

| Ore | Vanilla, blocks/chunk | Pack |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | 1083.0 | 1083.0 |
| Iron | 711.0 | 711.0 |
| Copper | 684.8 | 684.8 |
| Gold | 343.8 | 343.8 |
| Redstone | 67.2 | 67.2 |
| Lapis | 31.8 | 31.8 |
| Diamonds | 52.5 | 52.5 |
| Emeralds | 50.0 | 50.0 |
| Quartz (Nether) | 720.0 | 720.0 |
| Nether gold | 255.0 | 255.0 |
| Ancient debris | 2.5 | 2.5 |

   This is a calculation of "attempts in solid stone" with corrections measured on 4096 + 1024 chunks in 1.21.1. A real block count in a generated 26.3 world hasn't been done yet (see below).

## Checks to do in game (26.3 or Horizon)

1. **The pack loaded.** No `data pack` / `registry` errors in the log, `/datapack list` shows the pack.
2. **3000×7000 frozen mountains.** By the 1.21.1 benchmarks with default parameters (x8, erosion -0.1), 14 seeds out of 100 have a 7000×3000 area of frozen peaks, among them **seed 94, with the area near spawn**. How to check on Horizon:
   - create a world with seed 94 and the pack;
   - in `server.toml` → `[properties]` add `horizon-pregen = true`, restart;
   - `horizon-server cmd pregen start 5000` (a 10000×10000 square around spawn);
   - `/locate biome minecraft:frozen_peaks`, `/locate biome minecraft:snowy_slopes`, then look at the map (a minimap, Horizon's far rendering, or uNmINeD if it reads 26.3).
3. **Ore.** Compare two fresh worlds with the same seed, vanilla and with the pack: go deep into a mine or generate an area and count ore blocks. The amount should be close, but in the pack ore lies in rare big deposits, so a small area may have none at all. Compare over hundreds of chunks.
4. **Structures.** `/locate structure minecraft:swamp_hut` and `minecraft:desert_pyramid` from several places: they should be about 2.3 times closer than in vanilla.

`tools\benchmark.ps1` and the `DatapackBiomesToPng` mod were made for Fabric 1.21.1 and measure the old generator. Measuring biome size and ore in 26.3 itself needs that mod ported to 26.3.

## The simple pack `CustomWorldgen-26.3-simple` (recommended)

Terrain without 3D noise: the height at each point (x, z) comes from a simple formula, so the terrain itself can't have overhangs or floating blocks, and distant terrain (LOD) is cheap to compute.

- **Ocean.** The farther from land (continentalness noise), the deeper: from the shore (Y 62) to Y 26 at the edge of the deep ocean, then down to Y 6, where the floor stays flat.
- **Land.** Shores at ~Y 63, then almost flat plains: Y 66..69, noise of only ±0.5 blocks with a period of ~1000 blocks (very few contour steps).
- **Mountains.** Where erosion is low and it's land: up to +170 blocks above the plains (already at erosion -0.6), plus ridges and valleys (up to ±55 blocks, period ~500 blocks); mountain heights mostly Y 140..250, at most ~Y 290. Slopes are steeper in mountains (0.24 on average, 99% of points below 0.81); on plains 99% of points are below 0.16.
- **Water and the aquifer.** Horizon's aquifer (like vanilla's) only fills with water where `surface + 8` is below sea level. Vanilla terrain never lies between Y 55 and 63, but ours does, so the pack replaces `aquifers.surface_level` with `surface - 8` (the file `noise_settings/overworld.json`). Now every piece of land below Y 63 (shore, river bed, hollow) fills exactly up to Y 63, and there's no water above 63. Before that, shores and river beds stayed dry and the ocean was higher than the land.
- **Rivers.** Where the "weirdness" noise is close to 0 (the `river` biome) and the land is low (below Y ~80), a bed is cut down to Y 59. There are no rivers in the mountains.
- **Big caves.** Remain on 14% of the map, no closer than ~10 blocks to the surface. No tunnel caves (only rare classic carvers).
- **World height** is vanilla: Y -64..319.
- Ores in big deposits, witch huts and pyramids (14/4) are the same as in the main pack.

Biomes and terrain use the same noises (no coordinate shift), so an ocean biome = ocean on the map, and mountain biomes lie on mountains.

In 12 simulated seeds, a 120×120 km area always contains a continuous mountain region of at least 3000×7000 blocks (erosion below -0.45, land). Whether it matches `frozen_peaks`/`snowy_slopes` depends on temperature; that has to be checked in game.

Not confirmed: trees and shipwrecks that hang in the air in screenshots. Distant terrain (LOD) draws trees approximately (per the Horizon docs it is a "visual approximation"), so some may exist only in LOD. Check whether a tree stays in the air when you walk up to it and the chunk loads.

The pack is generated by `python build-pack-simple.py`. It takes ores, structures and carvers from `CustomWorldgen-26.3-lite`, so the lite pack (`build-pack.ps1 -Lite`) must be built first. The terrain parameters are in `tools/simple_terrain.py` and at the top of the script. `python tools/simulate_simple.py` shows slopes and the shares of ocean and mountains.

## The lite version for LOD

The `CustomWorldgen-26.3-lite` pack does the same (big smooth biomes, flat plains, big mountains, ores in big deposits, more huts and pyramids) but is cheaper for the generator that computes distant terrain (LOD):

- vanilla world height: Y -64..319 (instead of up to Y 655), terrain is generated up to Y 320 instead of 512;
- no tunnel cave noise (spaghetti, noodle, entrances): tunnels come only from rare classic carvers (about 30 times rarer than vanilla, i.e. almost none). Big cave halls remain;
- the other density functions are the same.

So the price of the simplification is almost no tunnel caves and no building above Y 319.

How to compare (same seed, a fresh world each time, LOD 32, `HORIZON_SPIKES=20`):

1. without a pack;
2. `CustomWorldgen-26.3`;
3. `CustomWorldgen-26.3-lite`.

Look at the share of `lod_detail` in the profile and the number of slow frames. If 2 is no worse than 1, the lag isn't caused by the pack. If 3 is noticeably better than 2, keep lite.

## The vanilla-surface pack `CustomWorldgen-26.3-vanilla-terrain`

For when you want caves, ores and structures as in `CustomWorldgen-26.3`, but **no changes to surface generation**: terrain, biomes, climate, rivers, oceans and world height (384) are vanilla.

```powershell
python .\build-pack-vanilla-terrain.py        # needs CustomWorldgen-26.3 built first
```

What's in it (all taken from `CustomWorldgen-26.3` unchanged):

- Tunnels only in their zones on 2.3% of the map, big caves on 14.1% of the map, carver tunnels 30 times rarer, canyons 10 times rarer.
- Ores in big single deposits, vanilla amount per chunk, noise ore veins (iron with tuff, copper with granite) off.
- Witch huts and desert pyramids about 5 times more often (a 14/4 chunk grid instead of 32/8).
- Ore doesn't touch air: all 25 ores have `discard_chance_on_air_exposure = 1.0`, so an ore block next to air isn't placed. Vanilla ore often peeks out in caves and on slopes; here everything hides inside stone. In caves and near the surface there is a bit less ore than the vanilla numbers; in solid rock the amount is the same (`ORE_AIR_DISCARD` in the script).
- Granite, diorite, andesite and tuff also in big veins: 10 blobs of 64 blocks instead of two separate ones (~3074 blocks per vein), with the vanilla amount of blocks per chunk (granite, diorite, andesite below Y 60 and above Y 64, tuff below Y 0). A vein can show in cave walls (`STONE_AIR_DISCARD = 0`). Dirt and gravel stay vanilla.

What it doesn't have: climate noises, `continents`, `erosion`, `ridges`, `offset`, `factor`, `depth`, `preliminary_surface_level`, `noise_settings`, `dimension_type`. `final_density` is the only file that touches the shape of the world: it's the vanilla one with the cave wrappers, and the top fade-out is back at vanilla Y 240..256. All 25 ores, 3 carvers and all the logic come from the full pack.

Don't put it together with other `CustomWorldgen*` packs in one world. The world must be new.

## Commands

```powershell
# (once) extract the vanilla 26.3 data from your own server.jar into _vanilla-26.3:
.\tools\Extract-Vanilla.ps1 -ServerJar C:\path\to\server.jar
.\build-pack.ps1                       # build CustomWorldgen-26.3
.\build-pack.ps1 -Lite                 # build the simplified CustomWorldgen-26.3-lite
.\tools\Check-Pack.ps1                 # check the format and references
```

The vanilla files (`_vanilla-26.3`) are Mojang's and are not in this repository: `Extract-Vanilla.ps1` creates them from your `server.jar` (~1150 files), and then `Check-Pack.ps1` also checks references into vanilla.
